= Extending percolate
:navtitle: Extending (SPI)

The consumer pages describe what percolate does out of the box. This page is for authors who want to teach
percolate something new. It is a map of the extension model, not a full contract reference — the
authoritative, versioned contract is the `spi` module.

== Everything is a strategy

Percolate's engine is deliberately small and knows nothing about specific containers or conversions. Each
mapping decision is made by a **strategy** discovered through the JDK `ServiceLoader`. The built-ins you
have been using — the `List`/`Set`/`Optional` handling, the constructor assembly, the method-call bridge
behind xref:conversion-methods.adoc[conversion methods] — are themselves just registered strategies, the
first customers of the same SPI you would use.

That means an extension is not a special case: you ship a strategy on the annotation-processor classpath and
percolate treats it exactly like a built-in.

Two principles keep extensions composable:

* **Strategies are myopic.** A strategy makes a local decision from the demand and the resolve context
  only. It never inspects or mutates the engine's graph — the engine owns all wiring. Needing graph access
  is a sign the decision belongs in the engine, not a strategy. This is not just a guideline: the engine's
  graph and stages live in an internal package the SPI never exposes, and an architecture test fails the
  build if a strategy implementation depends on it.
* **Over-emit, then prune.** A strategy returns *candidate* operations; the engine picks the cheapest
  viable plan by weight. You do not decide whether your candidate "wins".

== Two SPI roles: ExpansionStrategy and DirectiveReader

Producing a value and reading an annotation are two different jobs, so they are two different SPI roles:

[cols="1,3"]
|===
| Role | Job

| `ExpansionStrategy`
| Answers a demand for a value with a candidate operation, or says why it cannot. Never reads an annotation —
  it only sees the neutral `Directive` a `DirectiveReader` already assembled.

| `DirectiveReader`
| Translates the annotations it owns — built-in or third-party — into calls on `DirectiveSink`:
  `bind` (a target is produced, optionally from a source path), `input` (an author-declared configuration
  value, keyed by a name the engine never interprets), `scopeInput` (a mapper-method parameter becomes a
  named scope input), `constrain` (a demand-scoped admissibility rule) and `reject` (what the author wrote is
  malformed on your annotation's own terms). Percolate's own `@Map`, `@MapEnum` and `@Ambient` readers ship in
  `strategies-builtin` and use exactly this surface — nothing built-in is special-cased.
|===

Reach for `ExpansionStrategy` to teach percolate how to *produce* a value (a new conversion, a new container,
a new accessor). Reach for `DirectiveReader` to teach percolate a *new annotation* — your own mapping
vocabulary, read once per mapper method and turned into the same `bind`/`input`/`scopeInput`/`constrain`/`reject`
facts the built-in readers produce. The two compose: a reader publishes an `input` under a key of your
choosing, and a strategy of yours reads that key back off the `Directive` — see below.

=== Refusing with a reason

A strategy that recognises a demand as its own but cannot serve it returns `Offer.refusal(subject, message)`
instead of `Offer.of(spec)`. `Stream.empty()` still means "not mine" and produces no diagnostic; a refusal is
for the narrower case — "this demand is mine, and here is exactly why I cannot serve it". The `subject` is an
opaque positioning handle: a strategy never builds one, it only forwards a `Subject` it received from a
`DirectiveInput`, so the eventual diagnostic points at the token the author actually wrote.

=== `reject` for a malformed declaration, `constrain` for an inadmissible candidate

A `constrain` refuses *candidates*, so it is only ever heard when some strategy offers one — use it to veto
*how* a demand may be served. When the declaration itself is wrong ("these two members are mutually
exclusive"), call `reject(subject, message)` instead: it is reported verbatim as a permanent error whether or
not anything ever demands the path, which is exactly the case a malformed declaration tends to produce. The
built-in `@Map` reader enforces its own shape rules this way.

== Depending on the SPI

A strategy author needs only `spi` at compile time (it depends solely on the JDK and JavaPoet,
because `CodeBlock` is part of the contract — it does not drag in the annotations or the processor).
Versions are managed by the `bom` platform.

[source,groovy,subs="+attributes"]
----
dependencies {
    implementation platform('io.github.joke.percolate:bom:{percolate-version}')
    implementation 'io.github.joke.percolate:spi'

    // ServiceLoader registration
    compileOnly 'com.google.auto.service:auto-service-annotations'
    annotationProcessor 'com.google.auto.service:auto-service'
}
----

Consumers then add your artifact to their `annotationProcessor` configuration alongside the `percolate`
starter, and your strategy participates automatically.
